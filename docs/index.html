The Hidden Epidemic Inside Normal Weight

BMI is a weight ratio

It cannot distinguish subcutaneous fat (benign) from visceral/hepatic fat (pathological). Location is everything.

Clinical scores fail in normal BMI

Clinical risk formulas lose accuracy when body weight is restricted to the normal range — their training distribution assumption breaks.

Individual biomarkers in normal range

Each of the 14 biomarkers may individually appear within normal reference intervals. The pathology lives in the joint 14-dimensional distribution.

No routine, affordable alternative

FibroScan costs $300–$500/scan and is gated behind specialist hepatology clinics — inaccessible for primary care screening.

04 / Model Architecture

DA-SS-iVAE — Dual-Anchored Semi-Supervised Identifiable VAE

The architecture constrains the latent space so that Z₁ monotonically tracks insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and Z₂ monotonically tracks hepatic steatosis (FibroScan CAP). Coordinates cannot rotate or rescale under retraining — they are mathematically locked.

Hyperparameters

Latent dimension2
Encoder layers128 → 64 → 32
Total parameters14,842
Training epochs150 (ES ≈ 70–80)
Random seeds5 (reproducibility)
OptimizerAdam, lr=1e-3
Batch size128

Loss Function

L = LELBO
+ λ₁ · Lanchor-IR
+ λ₂ · Lanchor-CAP
+ λ₃ · ||Cov(Z)||F
λ₁=10.0 (IR anchor weight)
λ₂=5.0 (steatosis anchor)
λ₃=0.1 (disentanglement)

Identifiability Guarantee

Following Khemakhem et al. (2020), the prior p(z|u) is conditioned on demographic covariates u. Anchor networks apply Softplus weight projections, ensuring ∂Z₁/∂HOMA-IR > 0 and ∂Z₂/∂CAP > 0 everywhere — preventing latent axis rotation under retraining.

10 / Limitations & Future Work

Honest Limitations & What Comes Next

This is a research prototype. Here is exactly what it can and cannot do, stated plainly.

⚠ Real Limitations

No time-series data. NHANES is cross-sectional — every patient is measured once. The model cannot track how someone changes over time; it only recovers their metabolic state at a single point.
Trained on U.S. data only. All 1,477 participants come from NHANES — a U.S. nationally representative survey. We have not validated on independently collected cohorts from other countries.
FibroScan labels incomplete (~60%). The liver-fat anchor (Z₂) trains semi-supervised because FibroScan CAP values are only available for a subset. More liver-fat labels would make Z₂ anchoring stronger.
Not a clinical tool. This is a research prototype. It has not been through clinical trials or regulatory review and must not be used to direct real patient care.

→ What Comes Next

Longitudinal validation. Repeat-measurement cohorts (e.g., UK Biobank with follow-up visits) would let us test whether our latent coordinates actually track how a patient's metabolic state changes over months or years.
Non-U.S. external validation. Testing on cohorts from South Asia, East Asia, or Africa — populations with very different metabolic disease patterns — would reveal how well the model generalises beyond its training distribution.
More FibroScan labels. Partnering with hepatology clinics to collect liver-fat measurements for a larger fraction of patients would let us train the Z₂ anchor fully supervised, removing the biggest current uncertainty in the model.
11 / Limitations & Future Work

What We Know We Don't Know

Honest disclosure of current limitations alongside a concrete roadmap for future research.

⚠ Current Limitations

Cross-sectional data only. Every patient is measured exactly once (NHANES design). The model cannot predict patient trajectories over time — only recover their current hidden metabolic state.
Single-survey training source. All data originates from NHANES — a U.S. nationally representative survey. External validation on independently collected non-U.S. cohorts has not been performed.
FibroScan labels partially missing. The steatosis anchor is semi-supervised because FibroScan-derived CAP values are available for only ~60% of participants. Expanded liver biopsy or imaging data would improve Z₂ anchoring.
Geodesic paths are unvalidated longitudinally. The Riemannian intervention solver computes mathematically shortest paths on the population manifold — not clinically proven patient trajectories. Prospective trials required.
Research prototype only. No regulatory review. Not validated in clinical settings. Cannot and should not be used to direct real patient care.

→ Future Research Directions

Longitudinal validation. Apply LMSIS to prospective cohort studies (e.g., UK Biobank with repeat visits) to validate whether the latent coordinate tracks actual patient disease progression over time.
Multi-site external validation. Validate on independently collected, non-NHANES cohorts — especially in South Asia, East Asia, and Africa — where metabolic disease patterns differ substantially.
Expanded label availability. Partner with hepatology centres to acquire FibroScan CAP labels for a larger fraction of the training cohort, reducing reliance on semi-supervised missing-data imputation.
EHR and primary care integration. Adapt the model for automated ingestion from electronic health record systems — enabling routine, zero-cost metabolic screening at the point of care for all normal-BMI patients.
Regulatory pathway investigation. Evaluate the evidence requirements for FDA/CE submission as a software-as-a-medical-device (SaMD) pre-screening tool, identifying the required clinical trial design.
