"LMSIS — Latent Metabolic State Inference System"

"A deep generative model that recovers hidden metabolic risk from routine blood biomarkers in adults with a normal body weight."

"Karamjeet Singh · Sarandeep Singh · Ubaid Ahmad · Aiman Younus Dar"
"View on GitHub" "Read the Full Report" "Try the Live Demo"

"Millions of adults with a clinically normal body weight may carry hidden, concurrent insulin resistance and hepatic steatosis — a combination of metabolic dysfunction that routine screening, built around Body Mass Index, is structurally unable to detect. LMSIS is a semi-supervised deep generative model that learns a two-dimensional metabolic coordinate space directly from fourteen routine blood biomarkers, with each axis mathematically anchored to a real clinical reference measurement: insulin resistance and liver fat content. The system places every patient on this map, provides a calibrated confidence range specific to their risk group, and computes a route back toward a metabolically healthy position."

"The system reads all fourteen blood values together as a single pattern, rather than checking each one against a normal range in isolation. From that pattern, it produces two coordinates — one standing in for insulin resistance, the other for liver fat — each mathematically locked to a real, independently measured clinical test, so the coordinates always increase in the same direction as the disease they represent."

"The architecture is a Dual-Anchored Semi-Supervised Identifiable Variational Autoencoder (DA-SS-iVAE). Identifiability follows from conditioning the latent prior on demographic covariates, per Khemakhem et al. (2020). Each anchor network is constrained to strictly positive weights via a Softplus reprojection. The steatosis anchor trains semi-supervised through a binary availability mask, since FibroScan-derived labels exist for only a subset of participants. Per-subgroup uncertainty uses Mondrian conformal prediction, stratified by phenotypic quadrant, directly addressing the subgroup coverage impossibility result of Barber et al. (2023). Intervention pathways are geodesics on the Riemannian manifold induced by the decoder Jacobian."

"Is this system ready to be used on real patients?"

"No. This is a research prototype intended to demonstrate technical feasibility, not a diagnostic device. It has not undergone clinical trials or regulatory review, and using it to direct real patient care would be inappropriate at this stage."

"Why not just use a simpler model, like Random Forest?"

"On the same matched test split, Random Forest reaches a marginally higher raw correlation with liver fat (0.596 vs 0.542). But at the actual clinical decision threshold, this system reaches a higher AUROC (0.791 vs 0.766), while also providing a biologically anchored coordinate system, per-subgroup confidence calibration, and an interpretable decoder — none of which a single-output tree model can provide."

"How do you know the two coordinates actually mean insulin resistance and liver fat, rather than being arbitrary numbers?"

"Removing the anchor networks in a controlled ablation collapses the model's correlation with liver fat from 0.542 to 0.180 — direct evidence that the anchors, not coincidence, are what gives the coordinates their clinical meaning. Each coordinate is also mathematically constrained to increase only when its real clinical target increases."

"Could this just be overfit to one dataset?"

"It was evaluated on a separate group of patients from a survey cycle collected two years later, with zero participant overlap verified directly, across five independent training runs (ρ = 0.583 ± 0.027, every run exceeding the original estimate). It was separately evaluated on a real demographic subgroup outside its primary training composition, with consistent results."

"Does this work fairly across different ancestries?"

"Evaluated on a real cohort of 355 Non-Hispanic Asian participants, the model's implied risk threshold is substantially lower than the universal clinical cutoff — consistent with published ancestry-specific research. This is reported as a clinically important finding, not a limitation to hide."

"Why is the national estimate given with such a wide range?"

"That width honestly reflects how few patients in a national survey fall into this specific, narrowly defined subgroup — a property of the underlying sample size, not a flaw in the model. The estimate is never reported without that range."

"What is the single biggest limitation of this work right now?"

"Every patient in the dataset was measured exactly once. The system has not yet been shown to predict what happens to a real patient over time, only that it can recover a snapshot of their current hidden state."
