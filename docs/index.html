BMI only measures weight

It cannot tell healthy muscle or skin from dangerous visceral fat wrapping around internal organs. Location is everything.

Standard calculators fail

Traditional clinical calculators lose accuracy when weight is restricted to the normal range — their basic formulas break down.

Normal results are misleading

Individual lab values (like glucose) often appear normal in isolation. Early disease is only visible when analyzing all markers together.

Scans are too expensive

Specialist scans (FibroScan) cost $300–$500 and require referrals, making them impossible to use for routine patient check-ups.

04 / Model Architecture

DA-SS-iVAE — Dual-Anchored Semi-Supervised Identifiable VAE

The architecture constrains the latent space so that Z₁ monotonically tracks insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and Z₂ monotonically tracks hepatic steatosis (FibroScan CAP). Coordinates cannot rotate or rescale under retraining — they are mathematically locked.

Hyperparameters

OptimizerAdam, lr=1e-3

Optimization Goals

1. High-fidelity biomarker reconstruction
2. Predict Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR)
3. Predict Liver Fat (CAP)
4. Disentangle axes for interpretability

Clinical Anchoring

The model strictly forces the X-axis to track Insulin Resistance and the Y-axis to track Liver Fat. The coordinates are mathematically locked and will not rotate, ensuring true medical interpretability without complex latent shifts.

07 / Equity & Ancestry Analysis

Evidence for Ancestry-Specific HOMA-IR Thresholds

The universal HOMA-IR clinical cutoff of 2.5 misclassifies substantial fractions of every ancestry group as metabolically healthy. LMSIS recovers group-specific implied thresholds — all well below 2.5.

Implied IR Threshold by Ancestry vs. Standard Cutoff (2.5)

Every single group's true metabolic threshold lies substantially below the standard 2.5 limit. The difference across groups is highly significant (Kruskal-Wallis: p = 3.65×10⁻⁵).

09 / Key Contributions

Four Novel Technical Contributions

Each contribution is independently validated and addresses a distinct gap in metabolic screening.

01 / Axis Anchoring

Constrains the VAE brain so that axes correspond directly to HOMA-IR and liver fat, preventing coordinates from rotating during retraining.

02 / Mondrian CP Safety Net

Guarantees ≥90% clinical coverage boundaries for high-risk subgroups where standard calibration fails (from 81.6% to 90.4%).

03 / Riemannian Geodesic Solver

Computes the mathematically shortest, curved paths on the patient manifold to steer patients safely back to healthy ranges.

04 / Ancestry Cutoff Discovery

Replaces flawed universal clinical cutoffs with ancestry-stratified risk thresholds, showing they are all well below the standard 2.5 HOMA-IR limit.

10 / Limitations & Future Work

What We Know We Don't Know

Honest disclosure of current model limitations alongside a concrete roadmap for future research.

⚠ Current Limitations

Every AI model has boundaries. Here is exactly where our model is currently limited:

  • Cross-sectional data only: NHANES data represents a single snapshot in time. We cannot predict individual patient timelines or disease progression rate yet.
  • Post-COVID sensitivity: On 2021-2023 NHANES data, the liver-fat axis correlation drops from 0.58 to 0.33 due to pandemic-related lifestyle shifts.
  • Research prototype: This system is a proof-of-concept, not validated in clinical trials, and should not be used to direct real patient care.

→ Future Directions

Our roadmap to translate this research prototype into a practical medical utility:

  • Longitudinal validation: Partnering with prospective biobanks (like UK Biobank with repeat visits) to test if coordinates track progression over years.
  • Multi-site validation: Evaluating the model on non-U.S. cohorts to account for differences in baseline prevalence and ancestry.
  • Primary care EHR integration: Adapting the code for automated, zero-cost screening directly within electronic health records.

LMSIS demonstrates that clinically meaningful metabolic risk can be inferred from routine blood biomarkers using interpretable, uncertainty-aware AI.
