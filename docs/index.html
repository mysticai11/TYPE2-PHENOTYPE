01 / The Problem

The Hidden Epidemic Inside Normal Weight

Millions of normal-weight adults have hidden liver fat and insulin resistance. Standard screening misses them because it is built around body weight (BMI). They look healthy on the outside, but carry high cardiovascular and diabetes risks.

BMI only measures weight

It cannot tell healthy muscle or skin from dangerous visceral fat wrapping around internal organs. Location is everything.

Standard calculators fail

Traditional clinical calculators lose accuracy when weight is restricted to the normal range — their basic formulas break down.

Normal results are misleading

Individual lab values (like glucose) often appear normal in isolation. Early disease is only visible when analyzing all markers together.

Scans are too expensive

Specialist scans (FibroScan) cost $300–$500 and require referrals, making them impossible to use for routine patient check-ups.

04 / Model Architecture

DA-SS-iVAE — Dual-Anchored Semi-Supervised Identifiable VAE

The architecture constrains the latent space so that Z₁ monotonically tracks insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and Z₂ monotonically tracks hepatic steatosis (FibroScan CAP). Coordinates cannot rotate or rescale under retraining — they are mathematically locked.

Hyperparameters

Latent dimension2
Encoder layers128 → 64 → 32
Total parameters14,842
Training epochs150 (ES ≈ 70–80)
Random seeds5 (reproducibility)
OptimizerAdam, lr=1e-3
Batch size128

Loss Function

L = LELBO
+ λ₁ · Lanchor-IR
+ λ₂ · Lanchor-CAP
+ λ₃ · ||Cov(Z)||F
λ₁=10.0 (IR anchor weight)
λ₂=5.0 (steatosis anchor)
λ₃=0.1 (disentanglement)

Identifiability Guarantee

Following Khemakhem et al. (2020), the prior p(z|u) is conditioned on demographic covariates u. Anchor networks apply Softplus weight projections, ensuring ∂Z₁/∂HOMA-IR > 0 and ∂Z₂/∂CAP > 0 everywhere — preventing latent axis rotation under retraining.

09 / Key Contributions

Four Novel Technical Contributions

Each contribution is independently validated and addresses a distinct gap in the prior literature.

01

Clinically-Anchored Identifiable Latent Space

A 2D latent representation in which each axis is mathematically constrained to track real, verifiable clinical measurement (HOMA-IR and FibroScan CAP).

02

Mondrian Conformal Prediction

Per-subgroup calibration restoring conditional coverage to ≥90% for high-risk cohorts, addressing subgroup impossibility results.

03

Riemannian Geodesic Planner

Shortest-path computation on the decoder manifold translating lab values to the metabolically healthy zone.

04

Ancestry-Specific Threshold Discovery

Systematic computation of ancestry-stratified IR thresholds showing standard guidelines misclassify risks across all major groups.

10 / Limitations & Future Work

What We Know We Don't Know

Honest disclosure of current limitations alongside a concrete roadmap for future research.

⚠ Current Limitations

Cross-sectional data only. Every patient is measured exactly once (NHANES design). The model cannot predict individual patient trajectories over time.
Post-pandemic distribution shift sensitivity. Z₂ (liver fat axis) degrades severely on the post-pandemic L-cycle (ρ = 0.583 → 0.332) while Z₁ remains stable. Mondrian CP successfully preserved ≥90% coverage.
Research prototype only. No regulatory review. Not validated in clinical trials. Cannot be used to direct real patient care.

→ Future Research Directions

Longitudinal validation. Apply LMSIS to prospective cohorts (e.g., UK Biobank with repeat visits) to validate latent progression tracking.
Multi-site external validation. Validate on non-U.S. cohorts to account for differences in baseline prevalence and ancestry.
Clinical EHR integration. Adapt the model for automated, zero-cost screening at the point of care within electronic health records.
